# Conversion copy — headline, hero, CTA, trust

From a teaching segment where he contrasts the weak default version of each landing-page element against the version that actually converts. Apply these while designing the hero and key sections (step 5 of the method). The pattern is always **weak default → stronger choice**.

## Headline
- ❌ **Weak:** talks about the company's history, or a vague line like *"innovative business solution."*
- ✅ **Strong:** states **the target audience's core pain point and the measurable solution you deliver.**

Write the headline about *the customer's problem and the outcome*, not about you. Make the result concrete and, where possible, quantified.

## Hero visual
- ❌ **Weak:** trendy illustrations "because they look cool."
- ✅ **Strong:** **the exact product you're selling, or the end result** of the service you provide.

Show the real thing or the transformation — not generic decorative art. (This is the same instinct as the cinematic product-as-hero rule in imagery-and-layout: the product/result is the hero.)

## Call to action
- ❌ **Weak:** *"Contact us."*
- ✅ **Strong:** a CTA that **sets the next step for the buyer's intent** — e.g. *"Request a free strategy call"* or *"Request a growth audit."*

The CTA should name the next concrete, low-friction step that matches where the buyer is, not a generic catch-all. Specific verbs + a clear value for clicking.

## Trust signals
- ❌ **Weak:** all testimonials dumped **at the bottom** of the page.
- ✅ **Strong:** **inject trust signals above the fold, in the first section** — client logos, quantified results, or industry-specific proof.

Don't make visitors scroll to the end to find a reason to believe you. Earn trust in the first screen with proof that's specific to their world.

## Quick checklist for any hero
- [ ] Headline = audience pain + measurable solution (not company/feature talk)
- [ ] Visual = real product or real end-result (not decorative illustration)
- [ ] CTA = specific next step matched to buyer intent (not "Contact us")
- [ ] Trust signals visible above the fold (logos / numbers / proof), not buried

# Imagery & Layout — the cinematic build

This covers steps 4–5 of the method: generating imagery in Figma, and the section-by-section layout patterns that give his sites their cinematic, editorial feel.

## The imagery unlock: one photo → a full site

His standout claim and demo: a brand hands him **one product photo**, and he produces an entire site's worth of imagery without leaving Figma, using the **Higgsfield Figma plugin**.

From that single source image he generates:
- **Hero image** — the product placed in an atmospheric, cinematic scene.
- **Product shots** — clean isolated angles for the grid.
- **Alternate angles** — the same product rotated/re-lit for variety.
- **Lifestyle / atmospheric scenes** — product in a moody interior, with props (florals, fabric, light).
- **A promo video** — short motion clip of the product, generated in-canvas.

The lesson to carry into any stack: **don't accept a thin asset library as a constraint.** Generate the imagery the design needs. If you're working in code (not Figma), the equivalent is generating hero/lifestyle imagery with an image model and treating it as a first-class design input, not an afterthought.

### Imagery direction (non-negotiable for the look)
- **Cinematic & moody**, dramatic directional lighting. Think film still, not catalog.
- **Atmospheric context** — the product lives in a world (dark room + candle glow, floating grass island, galaxy). Never a bare cutout on pure white in the hero.
- **Tonally matched** to the palette from the Colors & Fonts deck.
- **Quiet luxury** — restraint and richness over busy or cheap-bright.
- Reserve **clean white/off-white isolated shots** for the product-grid section only, as the calm contrast beat.

If you have the `higgsfield-product-photoshoot`, `higgsfield-generate`, or `ai-image-director` skills available, use them to execute this imagery step — they're the production arm of this direction.

## Layout patterns — section by section

His sites follow a consistent narrative rhythm. Build in this order:

### 1. Loading / intro (optional)
A brief branded loader (e.g., CALMM showed a pill capsule animating in). Sets tone before the hero.

### 2. Hero — the cinematic statement
- **Full-bleed** background image or color.
- **Giant wordmark** in the display font — often oversized, layered behind or interacting with the product. The brand name *is* the art.
- A short, bold **editorial headline** ("Healthy Gut. Play Better.", "More Than Coffee.").
- One clear CTA ("Shop now", "Get Started"), understated.
- The **product, dramatically lit**, as the focal object.

### 3. Product / "Best Sellers" grid — the calm beat
- Switch to a **clean off-white** section.
- Products in a row/grid with clean isolated shots, name, price, small detail.
- Headings like "Check out our products", "Explore all our products", "Best Sellers Sets".
- This contrast (dark cinematic → light calm) is a signature; it makes both sections stronger.

### 4. Feature / ingredient / story strip
- Explain what makes the product special. CALMM listed ingredients (Probiotics, Green Tea Extract, Vitamin C…) beside the bottle; coffee showed "Our Fresh Menu".
- Often pairs a big product render with a tight bulleted/iconed list.

### 5. Mission / brand story
- A statement section in the brand voice, big type, generous space. ("At The Calmm, we're on a mission to bring life into every space…")

### 6. Social proof — "Real stories, real results"
- Testimonials, but arranged with personality — he favors a **circular / orbit layout** of avatars rather than a plain testimonial row.

### 7. Editorial / lifestyle break
- A full-bleed lifestyle image or big wordmark band (the VELOUR site repeated the wordmark over a cinematic scene as a divider). Gives the eye a cinematic pause.

### 8. Footer / CTA close
- Final call to action, wordmark, links. Keep it on-brand and quiet.

## Layout principles threaded through all of it
- **Type is big.** Headlines and wordmarks are the largest elements; don't be timid.
- **One idea per section.** Each section has a single job and lots of breathing room.
- **Alternate temperature.** Dark cinematic and light calm sections trade off down the page for rhythm.
- **The product is always the hero**, never decoration.
- **Negative space is luxury.** Crowded = cheap. Space = premium.
- **Editorial copy.** Short, confident, declarative headlines — adjective triplets ("Powerful. Pure. Gentle."), bold claims ("Radical Transparency. Hide Nothing."). Write copy like a fashion campaign, not a feature sheet.

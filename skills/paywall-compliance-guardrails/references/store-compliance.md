## 1. What MUST be disclosed on/near the paywall before purchase

### Apple

- **3.1.2(c) Subscription Information** — direct quote from App Store Review Guidelines: *"Before asking a customer to subscribe, you should clearly describe what the user will get for the price. How many issues per month? How much cloud storage? What kind of access to your service? Ensure you clearly communicate the requirements described in Schedule 2 of the Apple Developer Program License Agreement."* **[confirmed — primary]** — source: [App Store Review Guidelines §3.1.2(c)](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- **3.1.1** (free-trial-specific, non-subscription context but same disclosure logic): *"Prior to the start of the trial, your app must clearly identify its duration, the content or services that will no longer be accessible when the trial ends, and any downstream charges the user would need to pay for full functionality."* **[confirmed — primary]** — same source.
- Schedule 2 of the Apple Developer Program License Agreement (referenced by 3.1.2(c)) is described by secondary sources as requiring the exact legal boilerplate: *"This is an auto-renewing subscription... Your subscription will automatically renew unless it is canceled at least 24 hours before the end of the current period. Your account will be charged for the renewal within 24 hours prior to the end of the current period. You can manage and cancel your subscriptions by going to your account settings on the App Store after purchase."* **[uncertain — I could not extract readable text from the Schedule 2 PDF itself (`https://developer.apple.com/support/downloads/terms/schedules/Schedule-2-and-3-English.pdf`, fetched 2026-07-20, returned encoded/unreadable stream data); this exact wording is sourced from a secondary account]** — source: [Apple Will Reject Your Subscription App if You Don't Include This Disclosure — RevenueCat/Medium](https://medium.com/revenuecat-blog/apple-will-reject-your-subscription-app-if-you-dont-include-this-disclosure-bba95244405d). Confirm directly against the PDF before treating this as legally exact.
- Common practitioner summary of what reviewers check for (title/duration/price/trial/cancel-path) is **[uncertain — aggregator blog, not primary]** — source: [App Store Review Guidelines (2025): Checklist + Top Rejection Reasons — NextNative](https://nextnative.dev/blog/app-store-review-guidelines).

### Google Play

- Google Play Subscriptions policy, direct quote per WebFetch render: *"the cost of your subscription, the frequency of your billing cycle, the automatic renewal terms, whether a subscription is required to use the app, and any other material information about the subscription"* must be clearly disclosed, and *"Users should not need additional action to access this information."* **[likely — WebFetch-rendered text of the primary policy page, not hand-verified against raw HTML]** — source: [Subscriptions policy — Play Console Help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9900533?hl=en)
- Google Play general payments guidance: *"Developers must clearly and accurately inform users about the terms and pricing of their app or any in-app features or subscriptions offered for purchase. In-app pricing must match the pricing displayed in the user-facing Play billing interface."* **[likely — secondary WebSearch synthesis citing the primary Payments policy page]** — source: [Understanding Google Play's Payments policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10281818?hl=en)
- Loot-box/randomized-item odds disclosure (relevant if a paywall bundles randomized rewards): *"Apps and games offering mechanisms to receive randomized virtual items from a purchase including, but not limited to, 'loot boxes' must clearly disclose the odds of receiving those items in advance of, and in close and timely proximity to, that purchase."* **[likely — same synthesis]** — source: same Payments policy page above. Apple has the equivalent rule at 3.1.1: *"Apps offering 'loot boxes' or other mechanisms that provide randomized virtual items for purchase must disclose the odds of receiving each type of item to customers prior to purchase."* **[confirmed — primary]**.

## 2. Free trials & introductory offers — eligibility, one-per-user, disclosure

### Apple

- Trial/intro-offer disclosure duty is the same 3.1.1/3.1.2(c) language quoted above. **[confirmed — primary]**
- Eligibility rule, per WebSearch synthesis of Apple's own subscription-offer documentation: *"Each Apple ID is eligible for one introductory offer per subscription group, ever,"* and existing/lapsed subscribers who already redeemed an intro offer in that group are not eligible again; switching offer type (free trial → pay-as-you-go) does not reset eligibility. **[likely — secondary render of Apple's official "Set up introductory offers" and "Auto-renewable Subscriptions" pages, not independently fetched verbatim]** — sources: [Set up introductory offers for auto-renewable subscriptions](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-subscriptions/set-up-introductory-offers-for-auto-renewable-subscriptions/), [Auto-renewable Subscriptions](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/subscriptions/), and practitioner summary at [RevenueCat — Free Trials & Promo Offers](https://www.revenuecat.com/docs/subscription-guidance/subscription-offers).
- Non-subscription free-trial mechanism (3.1.1): must use a Non-Consumable IAP item at Price Tier 0 named "XX-day Trial," with duration/content-loss/downstream-charges disclosed before the trial starts. **[confirmed — primary]**

### Google Play

- Free trial duration bound: *"Free trials must be between 3 days and 3 years."* **[likely — WebFetch render of primary Play Console Help "Create and manage subscriptions" page]** — source: [Create and manage subscriptions — Play Console Help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/140504?hl=en)
- Eligibility control: Play Console offers an *"Allow one free trial per app"* setting; per WebSearch synthesis, once a user has used a free trial/introductory price on a given SKU, resubscribing to that same SKU no longer grants free trial/intro pricing. **[uncertain — this is a community/support-thread-level synthesis, not a directly quoted policy sentence; the exact scope (per-app vs per-SKU vs per-account) is not pinned down by a single authoritative quote]** — sources: [Create and manage subscriptions](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/140504?hl=en), community threads referenced in the same search batch.
- Pre-enrollment disclosure: *"clearly and accurately describe the terms of your offer, including the duration, pricing, and description of accessible content or services"* and users must understand when the trial converts to paid and how to cancel. **[likely — WebFetch render of the primary Subscriptions policy page]** — source: [Subscriptions policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9900533?hl=en)

## 3. Close/decline requirement — is a visible dismiss required? Rulings on hard-to-close paywalls

- **Apple has no single guideline sentence located in this research that says "a paywall must have a close button."** What was found:
  - Apple HIG guidance, per WebSearch synthesis of the (unreachable-by-direct-fetch) HIG "In-app purchase" pages: *"a modal sheet's default Close button makes it easy for people to return to free content with one tap, and if you create a custom sign-up view instead of using a modal sheet, you should design it to include a Close or Cancel button that lets people return to free content."* **[uncertain — my direct WebFetch of `developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/in-app-purchase` returned only a page title with no body content; this quote comes from WebSearch's own synthesis of that page and could not be independently verified verbatim in this session]** — source: [In-app purchase — Human Interface Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/in-app-purchase) (unverified render).
  - Separate HIG point on subscription-management discoverability, same caveat: *"if the manage subscription action is difficult to find or recognize, subscribers can feel they're being discouraged or prevented from canceling."* **[uncertain — same unverified-render caveat]**
  - Review Guideline 3.1.2(a) is the closest confirmed textual hook: *"Apps that attempt to trick users into purchasing a subscription under false pretenses or engage in bait-and-switch and scam practices... will be removed from the App Store and you may be removed from the Apple Developer Program."* **[confirmed — primary]** — this is the enforcement basis for "hard-to-close" dark-pattern rejections, but the Guidelines text itself does not use the words "close button" or "dismiss."
  - "Hard to close" as a documented rejection pattern (toggle defaulting a trial to ON, price shown small vs. large, cancel info buried) is reported only by aggregator/ASO blogs, not by Apple's own text: **[uncertain — no primary Apple source found for these specific described patterns]** — sources: [How to design a paywall for iOS that converts — Adapty](https://adapty.io/blog/how-to-design-ios-paywall/), [Common iOS Paywall Rejections — RevenueFlo](https://revenueflo.com/blog/common-ios-paywall-rejections-and-the-fixes-that-work), [Guideline 3.1 Rejection guide — iOS Submission Guide](https://iossubmissionguide.com/guideline-3-1-in-app-purchase/).

- **Google Play**: no rule found in this research that names a mandatory "close" button on the paywall screen itself. What is confirmed is the *post-purchase* cancellation-access requirement (see §4) and the general Deceptive Behavior prohibition on multi-screen flows engineered to trick users into subscribing:
  - *"Multiple screens in the purchase flow that lead users into accidentally clicking the subscribe button"* is listed as a prohibited deceptive-subscription practice. **[likely — WebSearch synthesis of Google's Deceptive Behavior / Subscriptions guidance, not independently fetched verbatim for this specific sentence]** — source cluster: [Deceptive Behavior policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9888077?hl=en) + [Subscriptions policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9900533?hl=en). Note: my direct WebFetch of the Deceptive Behavior page (below) did **not** surface this sentence — it surfaced five different sub-policies (misleading claims, device-settings changes, enabling dishonest behavior, manipulated media, hidden features) with no subscription-specific clause. **This is a direct contradiction between two of my own tool calls** — flagged, not resolved. Treat the "multiple screens" quote as unverified until checked live at the URL above.
  - Confirmed verbatim from my direct WebFetch of the Deceptive Behavior page: *"We don't allow apps that attempt to deceive users or enable dishonest behavior"*; *"We don't allow apps that contain false or misleading information or claims, including in the description, title, icon, and screenshots"*; *"Don't include any hidden, dormant, or undocumented features within your app. Techniques to evade app reviews are not allowed."* **[confirmed — primary, direct WebFetch render]** — source: [Deceptive Behavior — Play Console Help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9888077?hl=en)

**Net assessment for §3**: neither store's primary text, as retrieved in this session, contains an explicit sentence mandating "a visible dismiss/close control." The dismiss-button expectation is real in practitioner consensus and is enforced in practice via the broader anti-deception / anti-bait-and-switch clauses quoted above, but I could not pin an exact citable sentence requiring it from either company's primary documentation. Mark this sub-finding **[uncertain]** as a whole.

## 4. Restore purchases requirement

### Apple

- Guideline 3.1.1: *"Any credits or in-game currencies purchased via in-app purchase may not expire, and you should make sure you have a restore mechanism for any restorable in-app purchases."* **[confirmed — primary]** — source: [App Store Review Guidelines §3.1.1](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/). Note this sentence is short and does not spell out "button must be visible without scrolling" — that operational detail comes only from secondary sources below.
- Practitioner elaboration (button visibility, StoreKit restore API call, state handling for restoring/restored/nothing-to-restore) is **[uncertain — secondary, not in the primary Guidelines text I retrieved]** — sources: [Restore Purchases Button Missing — VP0 Journal](https://vp0.com/blogs/restore-purchases-button-missing-rejection-fix), [RevenueCat community thread on restore-purchases rejections](https://community.revenuecat.com/sdks-51/we-get-rejected-from-apple-store-review-about-restore-purchases-693).

### Google Play

- No explicit "restore purchases" clause was located in the primary Subscriptions or Payments policy text retrieved in this session. Google Play's architecture (purchases tied to the signed-in Google account across devices via Play Billing) makes an explicit restore-button mandate less central than on iOS; this is **[unverified]** — I found no primary Google Play policy sentence on this point and am not asserting one exists or doesn't beyond what was retrieved.

## 5. External-purchase / link-out rules after DMA (EU) and US Epic ruling — what's allowed now (2024–2025)

### United States (Epic v. Apple)

- January 2024, initial narrow entitlement: Apple introduced the **StoreKit External Purchase Link Entitlement (US)** under Guideline 3.1.1(a), letting qualifying US-storefront apps link out, but *"a commission will apply to digital purchases facilitated through the StoreKit Purchase Link Entitlement (US)"* under the initial rules. **[likely — secondary synthesis of Apple's Jan 2024 announcement and entitlement docs]** — sources: [Daring Fireball, Jan 2024](https://daringfireball.net/linked/2024/01/16/apple-guidelines-external-purchase-links), [InfoQ](https://www.infoq.com/news/2024/01/apple-external-purchases/), [Apple StoreKit External Purchase entitlement docs](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/bundleresources/entitlements/com.apple.developer.storekit.external-purchase-link).
- April 30, 2025 US District Court order (Epic v. Apple contempt ruling) removed the commission on external US transactions and the restrictive entitlement scheme: apps on the **US storefront** can now include buttons, external links, and other calls to action directing customers to purchasing mechanisms outside In-App Purchase, without an Apple commission on those external transactions. **[likely — secondary synthesis of court-ruling coverage, not the court order text itself]** — sources: [Neon Commerce — April 30 2025 Epic v. Apple ruling](https://www.neonpay.com/blog/making-sense-of-the-april-30th-2025-epic-v-apple-ruling), [RevenueCat — Apple anti-steering ruling](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/apple-anti-steering-ruling-monetization-strategy).
- This is corroborated by the **primary** Guidelines text I fetched directly: *"These entitlements are not required for developers to include buttons, external links, or other calls to action in their United States storefront apps... In all other storefronts, except for the United States storefront, where this prohibition does not apply, apps and their metadata may not include buttons, external links, or other calls to action that direct customers to purchasing mechanisms other than in-app purchase."* **[confirmed — primary]** — source: [App Store Review Guidelines §3.1.1(a)](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/). This primary text confirms the US carve-out exists and is current as of the guidelines page fetched 2026-07-20; it does not itself state the commission was removed (that detail rests on the secondary sources above).

### European Union (DMA)

- Apple's EU Link Entitlement lets developers link EU users to their own web storefronts to process payments with their preferred payment method. **[likely — secondary synthesis of Apple's own EU compliance pages]** — source: [Apple — Update on apps distributed in the European Union](https://developer.apple.com/support/dma-and-apps-in-the-eu/)
- April 23, 2025: European Commission found Apple in breach of DMA anti-steering obligations and ordered removal of restrictions on communicating/promoting external offers. **[likely — secondary press/legal-blog synthesis; the Commission decision itself was not fetched]** — source: [Coda — 2025 DMA Compliance: Apple's Out-of-App Updates](https://www.coda.co/blog/apple-dma-2025-updates/)
- June 26, 2025: Apple updated EU App Store terms — commission on in-app purchases reduced from 30% to 20%; external purchases now carry a layered fee structure reported as "2% initial-acquisition + 5–13% Store Services + 5% Core Technology Commission"; developers must choose IAP-only or external-links-only per EU storefront; an Apple-mandated disclosure screen warns users they are leaving the App Store. **[uncertain — multiple overlapping secondary sources with slightly different fee breakdowns; treat exact percentages as needing re-verification against Apple's live EU terms before quoting to a client]** — sources: [MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2025/06/26/app-store-eu-rule-change-dma/), [Daring Fireball](https://daringfireball.net/2025/06/apple_app_store_policy_updates_dma), [RevenueCat — Apple's June 2025 EU update](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/apple-eu-dma-update-june-2025/), [Neon Commerce](https://www.neonpay.com/blog/making-sense-of-apple-s-eu-app-store-terms-why-they-still-undermine-the-dma).

### Google Play (relevant, not asked for explicitly but adjacent to "external purchase" landscape)

- Google states it will **not** require Play Billing exclusivity or prohibit alternative in-app payment methods, and runs two US programs: an **Alternative Billing Program** (alternative billing system inside the app) and an **External Content Links Program** (link out to an external purchase destination). **[likely — WebSearch synthesis of Google's own announcement/policy pages]** — sources: [Android Developers Blog — Expanded billing choice, June 2026](https://android-developers.googleblog.com/2026/06/play-expanded-billing.html), [An update regarding Google Play's policies for developers serving users in the US](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/15582165?hl=en).
- Reported new service-fee structure: 10% starting fee on the first $1M annual earnings (applies to auto-renewing subscriptions too) regardless of billing method used, effective June 30, 2026 for US/EEA/UK. **[uncertain — single WebSearch synthesis pass; not independently cross-checked against a second source or the primary announcement page directly]** — source: same Android Developers Blog link above.

## 6. Common rejection reasons tied to paywalls (Guideline 3.1.1, metadata, deceptive UI)

All items in this section are **[uncertain — sourced from ASO/dev-agency blogs and community forum threads, not from an Apple- or Google-published "top rejection reasons" list]**, except where explicitly marked otherwise. Treat as directionally useful, not as citable policy text.

- **Price/period mismatch between paywall UI and App Store Connect metadata** — reported real-world example: monthly subscription listed at $4.99 in metadata vs. $5.99 actually charged in-app triggered rejection. Source: [Volpis — Common App Store Rejection Reasons](https://volpis.com/blog/app-store-rejection-reasons/).
- **Screenshots showing gated/premium features as if free**, or showing login/splash screens, or referencing another platform (e.g., mentioning Android) — reported as classic metadata-rejection triggers. Source: [screenshotbro.app](https://screenshotbro.app/blog/app-store-screenshots-rejected-fix), [Volpis](https://volpis.com/blog/app-store-rejection-reasons/).
- **Missing Restore Purchases control** — reported as a frequent automatic rejection tied to Guideline 3.1.1. Source: [VP0 Journal](https://vp0.com/blogs/restore-purchases-button-missing-rejection-fix).
- **Trial defaulted ON via a hidden/pre-toggled switch, price shown in small text while a larger/misleading number is emphasized, cancellation info buried** — reported dark-pattern rejection triggers under 3.1.1/3.1.2. Source: [Adapty — How to design a paywall for iOS](https://adapty.io/blog/how-to-design-ios-paywall/).
- **Missing in-app links to Terms of Use (EULA) and Privacy Policy** for auto-renewing subscriptions — reported as required *inside the app UI itself*, not just on the App Store product page or developer website. Source: [nextnative.dev](https://nextnative.dev/blog/app-store-review-guidelines). This aligns directionally with confirmed-primary Guideline language in §7 below but the "must be in-app UI, not just metadata" specificity itself is not from the primary Guidelines text I fetched.
- **Confirmed-primary enforcement hook** for all of the above: Guideline 3.1.2(a): *"Apps that attempt to scam users will be removed from the App Store. This includes apps that attempt to trick users into purchasing a subscription under false pretenses or engage in bait-and-switch and scam practices; these will be removed from the App Store and you may be removed from the Apple Developer Program."* **[confirmed — primary]** — source: [App Store Review Guidelines §3.1.2(a)](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)

### Google Play equivalent (confirmed primary)

- *"We don't allow apps that contain false or misleading information or claims, including in the description, title, icon, and screenshots."* **[confirmed — primary, direct WebFetch]** — source: [Deceptive Behavior policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9888077?hl=en)
- *"Don't include any hidden, dormant, or undocumented features within your app."* **[confirmed — primary, direct WebFetch]** — same source.
- SKU names like "Free Trial" or "Try Premium membership - 3 days for free" applied to a subscription with an auto-recurring charge, without clarifying the recurring nature, is reported as a specifically-named deceptive practice. **[uncertain — WebSearch synthesis, not confirmed by my direct WebFetch of the Deceptive Behavior page, which did not surface this example]** — source: [Wirefly — Google announces Play Store policy update to fight deceptive subscriptions](https://news.wirefly.com/2020/04/16/google-play-store-policy-update-fight-deceptive-subscriptions?amp=) (note: this is a 2020 article; re-verify it reflects current policy before citing as current).

## 7. Copy/terms boilerplate that compliant paywalls include

- Apple auto-renew legalese (as reported by secondary source, tied to Schedule 2 §3.8(b) of the Paid Applications Agreement): *"Your subscription will automatically renew unless it is canceled at least 24 hours before the end of the current period. Your account will be charged for the renewal within 24 hours prior to the end of the current period. You can manage and cancel your subscriptions by going to your account settings on the App Store after purchase."* **[uncertain — could not independently verify verbatim against the primary Schedule 2 PDF in this session; see §1]** — source: [RevenueCat/Medium](https://medium.com/revenuecat-blog/apple-will-reject-your-subscription-app-if-you-dont-include-this-disclosure-bba95244405d).
- Apple confirmed-primary requirement to include Terms of Use/Privacy Policy links and clear price/service description: Guideline 3.1.2(c) quoted in §1. **[confirmed — primary]**
- Google Play confirmed-primary requirement: cost, billing frequency, auto-renewal terms, whether subscription is required to use the app, "any other material information," plus an easy-to-use online cancellation method (Play Subscription Center link and/or in-app cancellation flow) surfaced in account settings. **[likely — WebFetch render of primary Subscriptions policy]** — source: [Subscriptions policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9900533?hl=en)
- Google Play confirmed-primary refund framing: *"If a user cancels a subscription purchased through Google Play's billing system, our general policy is that the user will not receive a refund for the current billing period, but will continue to receive their subscription content for the remainder."* **[likely — WebFetch render of primary policy page]** — same source.

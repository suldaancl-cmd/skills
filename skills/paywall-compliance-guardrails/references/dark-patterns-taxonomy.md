## 1. Full taxonomy of subscription-specific dark patterns

Primary taxonomy sources: Harry Brignull's [deceptive.design](https://www.deceptive.design/types) (successor to darkpatterns.org, the term he coined in 2010 — [Wikipedia: Dark pattern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dark_pattern)), the FTC's [*Bringing Dark Patterns to Light*](https://www.ftc.gov/reports/bringing-dark-patterns-light) staff report ([PDF](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/P214800+Dark+Patterns+Report+9.14.2022+-+FINAL.pdf)), and the academic taxonomy in Mathur et al., *[Dark Patterns at Scale: Findings from a Crawl of 11K Shopping Websites](https://webtransparency.cs.princeton.edu/dark-patterns/)* (CSCW/PACM HCI, 2019; [PDF](https://www.cs.umd.edu/class/spring2021/cmsc614/papers/dark-patterns.pdf), [GitHub data](https://github.com/aruneshmathur/dark-patterns)).

| Pattern | Definition | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **Forced continuity** | A free trial silently converts to a paid subscription with no reminder, and the card is charged without a fresh, affirmative confirmation. | Brignull's original 2010 taxonomy, cited in [deceptive.design](https://www.deceptive.design/types) and [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dark_pattern) |
| **Roach motel / "Hard to Cancel"** | "The user finds it easy to sign up or subscribe, but when they want to cancel they find it very hard." | [deceptive.design/types](https://www.deceptive.design/types) |
| **Hidden costs** | "The user is enticed with a low advertised price" but discovers unexpected fees only at checkout or billing. | [deceptive.design/types](https://www.deceptive.design/types) |
| **Sneak into basket / Hidden Subscription** | "The user is unknowingly enrolled in a recurring subscription or payment plan without clear disclosure or explicit consent," e.g. an add-on (insurance, warranty) auto-added unless opted out. | [deceptive.design/types](https://www.deceptive.design/types); example of opt-out add-ons per [BTLJ, "Trapped By Design"](https://btlj.org/2025/11/trapped-by-design-how-dark-patterns-manipulate-your-choices-and-the-regulators-fighting-back/) |
| **Confirmshaming** | Guilt/shame-based copy on the decline option (e.g. "No thanks, I don't want to save money") designed to emotionally manipulate the user into opting in. | Listed in Brignull's taxonomy; described as "emotional manipulation influencing user decisions" at [deceptive.design/types](https://www.deceptive.design/types) |
| **Disguised ads** | "Advertisements masquerading as native interface elements" so users click them believing they are content or navigation, not paid promotion. | [deceptive.design/types](https://www.deceptive.design/types) |
| **Preselection** | "The user is presented with a default option that has already been selected for them, in order to influence their decision-making" — e.g. the annual plan or a paid add-on ships pre-checked. | [deceptive.design/types](https://www.deceptive.design/types) |
| **Trick questions** | Double-negative or confusingly worded checkboxes/toggles that make the user's actual choice unclear (classic example: an unchecked box that actually means "don't unsubscribe me"). | Brignull's original 12-pattern taxonomy per [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dark_pattern) and [yukaichou.com summary](https://yukaichou.com/gamification-analysis/dark-patterns-brignull-manipulative-design-ux/) |
| **Fake urgency / countdown timers** | "The user is pressured into completing an action because they are presented with a fake time limitation." | [deceptive.design/types](https://www.deceptive.design/types) |
| **Fake scarcity** | False claims of limited supply/limited spots pressure users into immediate purchase. | [deceptive.design/types](https://www.deceptive.design/types) |
| **Bait-and-switch (trial-to-paid)** | User is enrolled expecting one thing (a free trial) and it converts into a materially different obligation (a paid, auto-renewing plan) without clear consent to that specific change. Apple explicitly names "bait-and-switch and scam practices" as grounds for removal. | [Apple App Store Review Guidelines §3.1.2(a)](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/) |
| **Obstruction** | "The user is faced with barriers or hurdles, making it hard for them to complete their task" — e.g. cancellation requires phone-only contact, long hold times, or a maze of pages. | [deceptive.design/types](https://www.deceptive.design/types); concrete instance in the [FTC v. Vonage](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2022/11/ftc-action-against-vonage-results-100-million-customers-trapped-illegal-dark-patterns-junk-fees-when-trying-cancel-service) complaint |
| **Nagging** | Persistent, repeated interruptions/pop-ups requesting an action the user has already declined (e.g. repeated upgrade prompts). | [deceptive.design/types](https://www.deceptive.design/types) |
| **Visual interference (tiny close button, low-contrast decline)** | Information or controls are "hidden, obscured or disguised" — e.g. a barely visible "X," a decline link styled as plain grey text next to a large colored "Subscribe" button. | [deceptive.design/types](https://www.deceptive.design/types) |
| **Comparison prevention** | Complex/obfuscated pricing or feature tables that make it hard to compare plans or compare against competitors. | [deceptive.design/types](https://www.deceptive.design/types) |

Mathur et al.'s empirical crawl of ~11,000 shopping sites independently found **1,818 dark-pattern instances spanning 15 types across 7 broader categories** (Sneaking, Urgency, Misdirection, Social Proof, Scarcity, Obstruction, Forced Action) — corroborating Brignull's categories via large-scale measurement rather than taxonomy alone. [Source](https://webtransparency.cs.princeton.edu/dark-patterns/) / [paper PDF](https://www.cs.umd.edu/class/spring2021/cmsc614/papers/dark-patterns.pdf).

The Norwegian Consumer Council's 2018 **[*Deceived by Design*](https://fil.forbrukerradet.no/wp-content/uploads/2018/06/2018-06-27-deceived-by-design-final.pdf)** report ([summary](https://edri.org/our-work/ncc-report-on-dark-patterns/)) — while focused on privacy settings in Facebook, Google, and Windows 10 rather than paywalls specifically — established the pattern categories later reused in subscription contexts: "privacy intrusive default settings, misleading wording, giving users an illusion of control, hiding away privacy-friendly choices, take-it-or-leave-it choices," and asymmetric friction (the privacy-friendly or decline path requires more clicks/effort than the exploitative one). This friction-asymmetry framing is now standard in subscription dark-pattern analysis.

## 2. Real enforcement actions

### United States — FTC

| Action | What happened | Amount | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| **FTC v. Amazon (Prime)** | FTC alleged Amazon used "manipulative, coercive, or deceptive user-interface designs known as 'dark patterns' to trick consumers into enrolling in automatically-renewing Prime subscriptions" and "knowingly complicated" cancellation (the internal process was reportedly nicknamed "Iliad," a multi-page cancel flow). Case filed June 2023, settled September 2025. | $1B civil penalty + $1.5B in consumer refunds (total $2.5B) | [FTC press release](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/09/ftc-secures-historic-25-billion-settlement-against-amazon); [original 2023 complaint](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2023/06/ftc-takes-action-against-amazon-enrolling-consumers-amazon-prime-without-consent-sabotaging-their); [Time coverage](https://time.com/7320708/amazon-prime-ftc-lawsuit-settlement-membership-subscription-cancel-dark-patterns/) |
| **FTC v. Vonage** | FTC alleged Vonage forced customers to cancel only via a live "retention agent" phone call (despite multi-channel signup), with obscured contact info, long/dropped calls, forced sales pitches, and surprise early-termination fees — while continuing to bill some customers even after they'd requested cancellation. Settled Nov 2022; refunds distributed Oct 2023 to 389,106 consumers. | $100M in consumer refunds | [FTC settlement release](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2022/11/ftc-action-against-vonage-results-100-million-customers-trapped-illegal-dark-patterns-junk-fees-when-trying-cancel-service); [refund release](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2023/10/ftc-sends-nearly-100-million-refunds-vonage-consumers-who-were-trapped-subscriptions-dark-patterns) |
| **FTC v. Age of Learning (ABCmouse)** | FTC alleged pre-checked enrollment boxes, auto-renew terms buried in hyperlinked T&Cs, and a cancellation flow that reset to step one if a user clicked any of the retention promos cluttering the "Cancellation Policy" page. Settled Sept 2020. | $10M | [FTC press release](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2020/09/childrens-online-learning-program-abcmouse-pay-10-million-settle-ftc-charges-illegal-marketing) |

**FTC "Click to Cancel" rule (Negative Option Rule) — current status [confirmed]:**
- The FTC's revised Negative Option Rule ("Click to Cancel," requiring cancellation to be at least as easy as sign-up) was **vacated by the Eighth Circuit Court of Appeals on July 8, 2025** — on *procedural* grounds: the FTC failed to publish a required preliminary regulatory analysis after determining compliance costs would exceed $100M. [Sidley](https://www.sidley.com/en/insights/newsupdates/2025/07/us-ftc-click-to-cancel-rule-struck-down); [Mayer Brown](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2025/07/click-to-cancelled-eighth-circuit-vacates-federal-trade-commissions-revised-negative-option-rule); [WilmerHale](https://www.wilmerhale.com/en/insights/client-alerts/20250801-eighth-circuit-vacates-the-ftcs-click-to-cancel-rule-but-federal-and-state-regulators-likely-to-remain-active)
- The underlying **1973 Negative Option Rule and ROSCA (Restore Online Shoppers' Confidence Act) remain in force** and were the actual legal bases for the Amazon and Vonage actions above. [WilmerHale](https://www.wilmerhale.com/en/insights/client-alerts/20250801-eighth-circuit-vacates-the-ftcs-click-to-cancel-rule-but-federal-and-state-regulators-likely-to-remain-active)
- On **January 30, 2026**, the FTC submitted a draft Advance Notice of Proposed Rulemaking (ANPRM) on negative-option plans to OMB/OIRA, signaling intent to restart rulemaking. [Subscription Insider](https://www.subscriptioninsider.com/article-type/news/ftc-restarts-negative-option-rulemaking-after-click-to-cancel-vacatur-signaling-subscription-rules-are-back-in-play)
- **FTC staff report "Bringing Dark Patterns to Light"** (Sept 15, 2022, 5-0 Commission vote): catalogued dark patterns across e-commerce, cookie consent, children's apps, and subscriptions; stressed that "dark patterns have a stronger effect when used in combination, rather than in isolation." [FTC press release](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2022/09/ftc-report-shows-rise-sophisticated-dark-patterns-designed-trick-trap-consumers) / [full report](https://www.ftc.gov/reports/bringing-dark-patterns-light)

## 3. What Apple and Google actually prohibit vs. allow

### Apple App Store Review Guidelines §3.1.2 (Subscriptions) — quoted directly

> **3.1.2(a) Permissible Uses:** "If you offer an auto-renewable subscription, you must provide ongoing value to the customer, and the subscription period must last at least seven days and be available across all of the user's devices... Apps that attempt to scam users will be removed from the App Store. This includes apps that attempt to trick users into purchasing a subscription under false pretenses or engage in bait-and-switch and scam practices; these will be removed from the App Store and you may be removed from the Apple Developer Program."

> **3.1.2(c) Subscription Information:** "Before asking a customer to subscribe, you should clearly describe what the user will get for the price... Ensure you clearly communicate the requirements described in Schedule 2 of the Apple Developer Program License Agreement."

Source: [developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/) (fetched directly, current text).

Schedule 2 of the Apple Developer Program License Agreement is separately referenced as containing the specific mandatory in-app-purchase disclosure requirements (title, length, price, trial terms, cancellation instructions), but this research pass could only confirm the *table of contents* of that agreement, not the full disclosure clause text — **[unverified in full text; cross-check https://developer.apple.com/support/terms/apple-developer-program-license-agreement/#S2 directly before quoting exact clause language]**. Secondary sources describe the practical requirement as: the billed price must be "the most clear and conspicuous pricing element," subscription title/length/price must be shown without requiring the user to scroll or tap a link, and terms must be presented "clearly and conspicuously... without requiring additional action from the user, such as opening a link." [RevenueCat community discussion](https://community.revenuecat.com/general-questions-7/app-review-rejection-guideline-3-1-2-ongoing-value-6617); general guideline text confirmed at [developer.apple.com](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/).

Apple's **interstitial-ad close-button rule** (a related but distinct clause, App Review Guideline 5.x on advertising) requires that "Interstitial ads or ads that interrupt or block the user experience must clearly indicate that they are an ad, must not manipulate or trick users into tapping into them, and must provide easily accessible and visible close/skip buttons large enough for people to easily dismiss the ad." **[Search-summarized paraphrase; verify exact clause number and wording directly against developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines before quoting as verbatim text.]**

### Google Play — Subscriptions policy

Confirmed requirements per [Google Play Console Help — Subscriptions](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9900533?hl=en):
- Developers "must clearly and explicitly disclose offer terms, the cost of subscription, the frequency of billing cycle, automatic renewal terms, whether a subscription is required to use the app, and any other material information about the subscription."
- Developers "must ensure their app clearly discloses how a subscriber can cancel and/or manage a subscription," and — where free trials/intro offers are used — "must clearly tell users how a user can cancel if they do not want to convert to a paid subscription."
- Users must be able to cancel at any time via Play Store; developers must *also* provide an in-app and on-website cancel option (deep-linking to Play Store subscription management is the recommended pattern).

Source also corroborated by [Android Developers Blog, "Building user trust through more transparent subscriptions" (2020)](https://android-developers.googleblog.com/2020/04/building-user-trust-through-more.html) and [Developer Android — Manage subscriptions](https://developer.android.com/google/play/billing/manage-purchases).

### Summary — prohibited vs. allowed

| | Apple | Google Play |
|---|---|---|
| **Prohibited** | Bait-and-switch / scam subscriptions; hidden pricing requiring scroll or link-click; subscriptions with no "ongoing value"; trials under 7 days | Undisclosed billing frequency/cost/renewal terms; cancellation paths that are hidden or harder than the Play Store's own path; failure to disclose trial-to-paid conversion terms |
| **Required** | Clear price/term disclosure in-flow without extra taps; Schedule-2-compliant terms; consistent value across devices | Explicit disclosure of cost, cadence, auto-renewal; clear in-app *and* web cancellation path; clear trial-cancellation instructions |
| **Allowed** | À la carte alongside subscription; upgrade/downgrade flows; bundling with cellular plans (with Apple approval) | Multiple offer tiers; introductory/trial pricing, provided disclosed |

## 4. The grey zone — tactics that convert but skirt the line

These are **not** confirmed as illegal per se in the US/EU sources reviewed, but are flagged by legal/design commentary as sitting in ambiguous territory between "friction" and "dark pattern":

- **Preselected annual plan as the default toggle position** — technically discloseable and reversible by the user, but exploits default-bias; the CCPA's statutory definition of a dark pattern — "a user interface designed or manipulated with the substantial effect of subverting or impairing user autonomy, decisionmaking, or choice" — is broad enough that aggressive preselection could qualify even without deception. [Consumer Financial Services Law Monitor summary of CA dark-patterns law, cited via BTLJ](https://btlj.org/2025/11/trapped-by-design-how-dark-patterns-manipulate-your-choices-and-the-regulators-fighting-back/)
- **Delayed/timed close button on upsell modals** — legal where a close control eventually appears and is findable, but commentary treats a *deliberately delayed* dismiss action as adjacent to "Obstruction" per Brignull's taxonomy. [deceptive.design/types](https://www.deceptive.design/types)
- **Trial-reminder timing** — sending the "your trial is ending" notice at a time/channel likely to be missed (vs. the FTC's implicit expectation, per the Vonage/ABCmouse actions, that renewal terms be salient) sits in a legal grey zone: nothing in ROSCA specifies *when* a reminder must fire, only that consent to the negative option itself must be clear. [FTC v. Vonage complaint background](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2022/11/ftc-action-against-vonage-results-100-million-customers-trapped-illegal-dark-patterns-junk-fees-when-trying-cancel-service)
- General framing: legal commentary (Berkeley Technology Law Journal, Nov 2025) notes dark patterns "capitalize on predictable human responses" and remain effective "even when information is 'technically disclosed'" — i.e., disclosure compliance does not by itself neutralize manipulative framing, which is exactly the grey zone regulators are now trying to reach via broader "autonomy impairment" standards (DSA Art. 25, CCPA). [BTLJ, "Trapped By Design"](https://btlj.org/2025/11/trapped-by-design-how-dark-patterns-manipulate-your-choices-and-the-regulators-fighting-back/)

## 5. Ethical and business case against dark patterns

- **Refund/chargeback pressure:** cancellation-refund requests average roughly **12%** of subscription volume, and **35% of cardholders** describe cancelling a subscription as "somewhat" or "very" difficult — friction that pushes disputes toward chargebacks rather than refunds: **84%** of customers say filing a chargeback feels easier than requesting a refund, and **52%** don't contact the seller before disputing. **[unverified — figures are drawn from an aggregator/industry-blog search summary (marketingltb.com, empirestats.net) rather than a primary payments-industry report (e.g., Visa/Mastercard chargeback data, Recurly, Chargebacks911); treat as directional, re-verify against a primary source before using in a client-facing deck]**
- **Churn dynamics:** involuntary churn (failed payments, not deliberate cancellation) is estimated at **20–40%** of all subscription churn — a reminder that dark patterns aimed at *voluntary* churn reduction don't address the larger failed-payment problem and can co-mingle with it to inflate complaint/chargeback rates. **[unverified — same aggregator-search-summary caveat as above]**
- **Regulatory/reputational risk is now directly quantified:** the Amazon settlement ($2.5B total) and Vonage settlement ($100M) demonstrate that dark-pattern subscription flows now carry FTC enforcement risk at a scale that dwarfs any short-term retention lift — both are [confirmed] via primary FTC press releases cited in Section 2.
- **EU exposure scales with global revenue:** the Omnibus Directive's up-to-4%-of-turnover fine ceiling for cross-border consumer-law breaches means dark-pattern paywalls are a board-level P&L risk in any company with meaningful EU revenue, not just a UX quibble. [EUR-Lex 2019/2161](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2019/2161/oj/eng)
- **Platform risk:** both Apple and Google explicitly reserve app removal / developer-account termination for subscription bait-and-switch — an existential distribution risk, not just a fine. [Apple Guidelines §3.1.2(a)](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/); [Google Play Subscriptions policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9900533?hl=en)

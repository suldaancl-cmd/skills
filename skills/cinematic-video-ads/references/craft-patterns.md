# Cinematic Craft Patterns

The grammar that makes an ad *feel* cinematic and emotionally grip the viewer. Pull these when designing shots and the emotional arc. Every pattern is sourced.

## Craft Patterns

**1. The Sensory Pre-Interrupt (visual hook).** Open with a stimulus that forces neural processing before the brand appears — unexpected texture, disorienting scale, motion-vs-stillness contrast. How-to: frame 1 = foreground element in sharp focus against a blurred unexpected background, or open mid-motion. Why: an anomaly the brain can't classify triggers involuntary attention. [ASH Media; Promo.com]

**2. In Medias Res Opening.** Drop the viewer into action/emotion already in progress; stakes before geography/name/context. How-to: frame 1 is action or consequence, not setup; context arrives at beat 3–5. Why: eliminates the "contract-signing" phase — the viewer is already committed when stakes are declared. [Swng Productions; Guinness "Surfer", Jonathan Glazer 1999]

**3. Metaphorical Logic (product-truth mirroring).** Build the visual narrative as a metaphor for one core product truth, so the brand need not be stated — the audience arrives at it. How-to: identify a single irreducible product truth (Guinness: good things take time), then find a real-world ritual that enacts it (surfers waiting for the perfect wave). Why: the viewer produces the brand equation themselves → ownership + memorability. [Guinness "Surfer" oral history, creative.salon]

**4. Kinetic Rhyme Editing (motion-match cut).** Cut between shots where the arc/direction/silhouette of motion continues across the edit. How-to: script shots with matching exit/entry trajectories, align on the apex. Nike "You Can't Stop Us" (W+K, 2020) ran 4,000+ clips through motion-analysis to find 36 morphologically identical pairs, cut as a single motion. Why: the eye tracks motion continuity and perceives one unified action; the edit becomes invisible and the emotional payload lands without argument. [workingnotworking; designboom]

**5. Tension–Plateau–Release Arc.** Structure runtime as escalating tension → one extended held beat of near-silence/stillness → explosive release. How-to: map music and cut-rate together; the plateau drops cut frequency to zero and ambient sound 6–10 dB. Why: physiological arousal then relief = strongest emotional memory trace (same as the musical "drop"). [Avid; Guinness "Surfer"]

**6. Shot-Scale Cascade (wide→intimate).** Open wide (world), move to medium (character in environment), arrive at extreme close-up (emotional truth — eye, hand, material). How-to: a deliberate three-act compression within the first 10–15s. Used by luxury auto (BMW, Mercedes). Why: the eye reads wide shots as information, close-ups as empathy — the cascade trains the viewer to admire *and* identify. [StudioBinder; autoads]

**7. Long-Lens Compression + Shallow Focus.** Shoot key emotional moments at 85mm+ wide aperture: subject sharp against a compressed, blurred environment. Why: shallow depth of field is a perceptual cue that says "this matters" — decades of prestige film trained viewers to read lens compression as intimacy/consequence. [Artlist; SIGMA Blog]

**8. Monochromatic Grade with Strategic Color Reserve.** Desaturate to near-monochrome, reserving one color for a specific object/moment (often the brand color). Why: color contrast in a desaturated field is among the most powerful attention directives — it codes the colored element as the meaning of everything else. [beforesandafters; CineD]

**9. Deferred Brand Reveal (earned logo).** Withhold brand/logo/product until the final 3–5s, after emotional investment is established. How-to: sustain on story/character/image; at peak emotional resolution cut to stillness and introduce the mark in silence or one tone. Why: the audience already accepted the emotional world — the mark lands as confirmation, not interruption. (Apple "1984"; John Lewis "Man on the Moon".) [lbbonline; D&AD 2016]

**10. Sensory Layering (sound–image counterpoint).** Pair a visual track and a sonic track that share emotional register but diverge in literal content. How-to: select music first, then edit image to counterpoint (not illustrate) the sound. Why: counterpoint engages more cognitive bandwidth than illustration — the brain completes the connection → deeper encoding. [soundtreemusic; creative.salon]

**11. Strategic Silence Drop.** Insert 1–2s of near-total audio silence at a structural pivot (all sound to −60 dB for one beat after a visual peak), before the next motion/soundtrack resumes. Why: in a perpetually noisy feed, silence is a pattern interrupt at the sound level; most effective when the following sound is the brand's sonic identity. [Zanna Sound; LBBOnline]

**12. Conflict-as-Character-Engine.** The protagonist is defined by what they overcome, not described. How-to: write the brief as [protagonist] + [one obstacle that tests the value you want the brand to own] + [transformation]. Nike "Dream Crazy" (W+K, 2018). Why: conflict generates tension + identification, and positions the brand as the enabling force without stating it. [D&AD 2019; Cannes Lions jury notes via Zappi]

## Mini-Frameworks

**The Metaphor Mirror** — [Product truth] → [Real-world analog that enacts it at epic scale] → [Brand appears as the reward/conclusion]. Rule: the metaphor must be internally coherent — the viewer should reconstruct the product truth from the story alone. (Guinness "Surfer", Apple "1984")

**The Emotion-First Cascade** — Emotion → Identification → Information → Brand. Anti-pattern: Information → Brand → Emotion (the standard TV ad; fails cinematic grammar). (John Lewis, Nike "Dream Crazy")

**The Tension-Economy Model** — Build (escalating edit rate + rising music) → Plateau (stillness + silence) → Release (payoff + brand). Ratio ≈ 60% build / 10% plateau / 30% release.

**Shot-Grammar Hierarchy for Prestige Film** —
1. Wide (world) — 2–4s
2. Medium with camera movement (character in world) — 4–15s
3. Extreme close-up (material/emotional truth) — 15–30s
4. Montage (action, rhythm) — 30s→(runtime−10)
5. Hold (stillness) — (runtime−10)→(runtime−5)
6. Logo/product reveal — final 3–5s

**The Counterpoint Stack** — stack three independent symbolic systems (image, music, voice/text) that share an emotional register but don't literally illustrate one another; each adds meaning rather than repeating.

> ⚠️ Cinematic is a STYLE, not a STRUCTURE. For lead-gen these patterns must be married to the conversion structure in `conversion-rules.md` — beautiful drone shots with no curiosity gap in the first 3s still lose 40% of viewers.

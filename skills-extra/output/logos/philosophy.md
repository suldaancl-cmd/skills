# Obsidian Alchemy

## A design philosophy for marks that behave like small, dense objects.

### I. The hand, not the filter

This is work made the way a goldsmith works: slowly, by candlelight, removing rather than adding. The mark is not *designed* in the software-catalog sense; it is *hammered* — struck repeatedly until nothing non-essential survives. Every intersection is meticulously crafted. Every angle is the product of deep expertise and painstaking attention — an edge refined until it could cut paper. The viewer should feel, without being told, that someone at the absolute top of their field sat with this for a very long time.

### II. Matter before message

Two substances, and only two: a deep, absorbent black — the colour of cooled basalt, of a room with the lights off — and a warm metal that begins at pale honey-gold near the top and dims to oxidised copper at the bottom. The metal is never flat. It behaves like a real surface under real light: a thin sheen where the light catches, a shadow where it folds away. This is not a gradient picked from a menu; it is the result of master-level execution, layered the way a patina accrues, so the mark reads like a physical artefact caught in a photograph, not a vector file.

### III. Silence as material

Space is the third substance, more generous than either of the other two. The mark sits inside a field of darkness that is deliberately, almost wastefully large — the mark never fills the canvas. Restraint is the loudest gesture available here. Negative space is not a leftover; it is curated, measured, and defended. A lesser practitioner would fill it. The master leaves it alone.

### IV. Angles, not curves

Form is built from straight edges meeting at precise, chosen angles — never the lazy forty-five, never the accidental. The geometry has been tuned the way a luthier tunes a bridge: one degree off and the whole piece goes dull. A single vertical anchors the composition; two diagonals intersect it with the rigour of a well-set jewel. Cuts are sharp, terminals are clean, the mark holds together at one centimetre and at one metre. Nothing is cute. Nothing softens to flatter the viewer.

### V. Typography as witness, not narrator

When text appears, it speaks in a whisper — small, widely tracked, set in a geometric face that has been drawn with the same discipline as the mark itself. Text exists to witness the mark, not to explain it. A wordmark sits beside the monogram at a respectful distance, never crowding. A founding year is set so small it reads as a quiet provenance stamp, the way a silversmith signs the underside of a bowl. No italics, no ornament, no temptation to perform.

### VI. The discipline of a closed hand

Obsidian Alchemy refuses everything a cheap logo reaches for: drop shadows, glows, bevels, chrome filters, decorative flourishes. It refuses novelty for novelty's sake. What remains — black, slow metal, patient space, angular form, whispered type — is made to survive scrutiny. Stare at it for five minutes and it should still hold. That is the test, and the only one that matters. Every element the work of countless refinements, every detail the result of painstaking craft, every mark a small, dense object built to outlast the taste of its moment.
